# Periodic Halo Orbit Finder

## Project Overview

The Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope is NASA's newest space-based observatory similar to Hubble or James Webb. It's a wide field infrared survey telescope built to map large areas of sky quickly rather than study small fields in depth. Primary science objectives include studying the effects of dark energy; searching for more exoplanets via gravitational microlensing and its coronagraph for direct imaging; and probing the universe for primordial black holes. It uses a mirror around the same size as Hubble's but with a field of view more than 100 times larger.

It's parking spot is known as Lagrange point 2 (L2), where the gravitational forces from the Sun and Earth cancel. At L2, the Sun and Earth’s gravity, combined with the spacecraft’s motion around the Sun, keep it lined up with Earth with little fuel use. The location also provides very stable optical performance, a largely unobstructed view of the sky, and reduced heating from the Earth, Sun, and Moon which matters significantly for an infrared telescope. Unlike low Earth orbit, there are no thermal cycles from day/night passes. Roman will remain in a quasi-periodic halo orbit during its operational lifespan.
